Veterans Treatment Court Series

From One Courtroom to a National Movement

CourtBridge Solutions

One courtroom. One veteran. One idea that would change how the justice system responds to those who served.

On January 4, 2008, Judge Robert Russell established the nation's first Veterans Treatment Court in Buffalo, New York.

The idea grew from something deeply human: veterans often respond differently when supported by people who understand military service, structure, sacrifice, and the challenges of returning home.

The Buffalo model brought together the court, treatment providers, the Department of Veterans Affairs, community partners, and volunteer veteran mentors. The goal was to hold participants accountable while addressing service-related conditions that may have contributed to their involvement with the justice system.

It was not an escape from accountability.

It was accountability with structure, treatment, supervision, and connection.

The idea quickly began moving across the country.

2008
Buffalo, NY — Judge Robert Russell establishes the nation's first Veterans Treatment Court
2009
Mansfield, OH (Richland County) — Judge Jerry Ault establishes Ohio's first Veterans Treatment Court
2010
Okaloosa County, FL — Judge T. Patt Maney establishes Florida's first Veterans Treatment Court
2012
Florida law formally authorizes Military Veterans and Servicemembers Court Programs statewide

In 2009, Judge Jerry Ault established Ohio's first Veterans Treatment Court in Mansfield — making Richland County one of the earliest communities in the nation to embrace this new approach.

The Mansfield program brought attorneys, probation officers, treatment organizations, and veteran mentors together to develop individualized plans for participants. The expectations were significant, but participants were no longer expected to navigate recovery, reintegration, and the justice system alone.

Florida joined the movement in 2010 when retired Army Brigadier General and Judge T. Patt Maney established the state's first Veterans Treatment Court in Okaloosa County.

In 2012, Florida law formally authorized judicial circuits to establish Military Veterans and Servicemembers Court Programs for veterans and servicemembers facing service-related substance use, mental health, or psychological challenges.

From Buffalo to Mansfield to Okaloosa County, the message was becoming clear:

The courtroom could remain firm.

The expectations could remain high.

Public safety could remain central.

And the justice system could still recognize the person — and the service — behind the case.

Veterans Treatment Court was built around an important question:

What if accountability and understanding could exist in the same courtroom?

That question became a national movement.

Next, we will look at what participation in Veterans Treatment Court actually requires — because this is not an easy way out. It is a structured path forward.
