Domestic Violence Data Series

The Tactics Are Often Invisible. The Impact Is Not.

CourtBridge Solutions

Alongside its intimate partner violence findings, the CDC's National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey (2023/2024) also released a dedicated brief on stalking — using the same data collected from September 2023 through September 2024.

1 in 5
women — approximately 28.8 million — have experienced stalking during their lifetimes
1 in 20
women — 7.0 million — were stalked in the 12 months before the survey
1 in 10
men — about 11.9 million — have experienced stalking in their lifetimes
98.7%
of female stalking victims felt afraid, threatened, or concerned for their safety or the safety of others

Most female victims (85.2%) suffered mental or emotional harm as a result.

The CDC found that technology-facilitated tactics — using GPS devices, apps, or stalkerware to track a victim's location or monitor their communication without permission — were reported by roughly 16% to 29% of victims, representing millions of people.

The CDC cautions that changes to survey methodology mean these findings should not be directly compared with estimates from previous NISVS survey years.

Stalking is frequently a warning sign in intimate partner violence cases, not a separate issue from it. Understanding how perpetrators use surveillance and technology matters for how courts, providers, and advocates assess risk.

Source

CDC, National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey: 2023/2024 Stalking Data Brief
